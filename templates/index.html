International Space Station Details

Permanent human outpost in Low Earth Orbit

  • Full Name: International Space Station (ISS)
  • Type: Modular Space Station / Microgravity Orbital Platform
  • Orbit Regime: Low Earth Orbit (LEO) • 51.64° Orbital Inclination
  • Mean Altitude: ~408 km (253 miles) above Earth's sea level
  • Orbital Velocity: ~27,600 km/h (17,150 mph) • ~7.66 km per second
  • Orbital Period: ~92.68 minutes per complete Earth revolution
  • Primary Purpose: Multidisciplinary scientific research in biology, human physiology, physical sciences, materials science, and Earth observation under sustained microgravity.
  • Habitation & Crew: Continuously inhabited since November 2000; typically hosts an international crew of 7 astronauts and cosmonauts.

ISS Visibility Information

Solar illumination and naked-eye ground sighting science

Daylight Solar arrays actively generating power

The ISS is currently on the sunlit side of Earth. Its massive solar arrays (spanning over 73 meters) are generating up to 120 kilowatts of electrical power.

Why 16 Sunrises Every Day?

Because the ISS circles the Earth once every ~93 minutes at 27,600 km/h, the onboard crew witnesses approximately 16 sunrises and 16 sunsets in a single 24-hour cycle. Roughly 45 minutes of each orbit is spent in daylight, followed by 45 minutes in Earth's shadow.

How to Spot the ISS from Earth?

The space station has no bright external spotlights visible from ground. Instead, it is visible to the naked eye because it reflects sunlight like a very bright moving star (often brighter than Venus). Sighting is possible during dawn or dusk: when your location is dark, but the station at 400 km altitude is still illuminated by the Sun.
